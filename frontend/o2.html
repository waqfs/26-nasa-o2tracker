E15: Everglades water-level change with and without NISAR phase Exploratory

Water Conservation Area 3A, Florida. We withheld each marsh gauge in turn and estimated its 12-day water-level change two ways: from the other gauges alone, and with NISAR interferometric phase calibrated by those same gauges. Both estimates were then compared with what the withheld gauge measured.

    How to read these measurements

      Methods and sources

      O2tracker makes NASA NISAR radar observations explorable: real acquisitions, a checked before/after comparison, interferometric coherence and phase, and an exploratory test of what radar adds to wetland water-level estimates. It is not an oxygen sensor, hypoxia predictor or early-warning service.

      Status labels

      Observed
      Direct satellite or field measurements (radar backscatter, coherence, phase, gauge stage, probe oxygen).
      Derived
      Quantities computed from measurements (backscatter difference, gauge-calibrated water-level estimates).
      Modeled
      Outputs of an explicit model or simulation (the educational oxygen-budget experiment).
      Exploratory
      Findings that still need independent replication or validation.

      What NISAR measures

      NISAR is an L-band synthetic aperture radar (wavelength about 24 cm). It sends its own microwave pulses, so it observes day and night and through cloud.

      Backscatter is how much energy returns to the satellite; it changes with surface roughness, vegetation and water. A change in backscatter is not by itself a flooding change.

      Coherence (0–1) shows how similar the radar echo stayed between two passes 12 days apart. Unwrapped phase records path-length change along the radar's line of sight; one 2π cycle is about 11.9 cm.
