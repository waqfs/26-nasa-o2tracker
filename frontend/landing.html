Radar doesn't take a photo. It measures how the ground answers back.

NISAR sends its own microwave pulses and listens for the echo, so it sees at night and through cloud. Wet ground, bare soil and vegetation each return a different amount of energy. That is why one place can look different five weeks apart.

A change in the echo is a clue, not a verdict. It isn't automatically flooding, and we say so every place it appears. Our question is narrower and more useful: where could radar add something that ground sensors alone cannot?

We tested whether radar helps estimate water level between gauges.

In the Everglades, we hid each marsh gauge in turn. We estimated its 12-day water-level change from the other gauges alone, and again with NISAR phase added. Then we checked both against what the hidden gauge actually measured.
